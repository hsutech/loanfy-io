When Borrowers Don't Know Which Loan Product to Choose

By Loanfy Editorial Team · September 29, 2026

In this article

  2. Ask for timing before product labels
  3. Show why a path is suggested
  4. Keep the comparison honest
  5. Design a reversible intake

Many lending journeys ask for a product selection before the borrower knows which product fits. The site presents “term loan,” “line of credit,” “equipment financing” and “cash advance” as choices, then waits for the visitor to click. That is efficient for people who already understand the differences. It can be a dead end for the person who knows only the business problem: a second truck is needed, invoices will be paid later, or a busy season requires inventory now.

A small business financing question on Reddit makes the gap visible. The author describes a landscaping company and conflicting advice about where to apply. This is one self-reported account, not a representative study or evidence of any lender's performance. For a product team, it is a useful prompt: what would an intake flow ask before it requires a borrower to know the product name?

Start with the job the money must do

The opening question should ask what the borrower is trying to change. “Buy a specific asset,” “cover costs until customer payments arrive,” “refinance an existing obligation” and “fund a project with an identified exit” produce more useful signals than a dropdown of product names. An applicant buying a truck may need to upload a seller quote; an applicant with receivables may need customer invoices. The answer shapes the evidence required later.

The flow should also allow a borrower to say “I am not sure.” Forcing a guess can send the applicant down an irrelevant path and create rework for the team. A better interface can show a short explanation of each use category and let the applicant revise it. The purpose is to gather enough information for a responsible next step, not to promise that an algorithm can underwrite the request from a single answer.

For business financing, the amount and use of funds should be separated. A visitor may enter a round number before adding taxes, setup costs or an operating reserve. Ask what the money will pay for and when. The SBA's Lender Match preparation guidance similarly emphasizes amount and use of funds, business history, projections and collateral before lender discussions. Lender Match itself is not a loan application or approval, and a private platform should be just as clear about what stage a visitor has reached.

Ask for timing before product labels

Timing can distinguish a long-lived asset from a short cash-flow gap. Ask when funds are needed and what dated event makes that deadline real. A seller may hold a vehicle for two weeks; a contractor may need materials before a signed project starts; an invoice may be due from a customer in sixty days. “As soon as possible” is not enough to assess whether speed is worth an additional cost.

Ask when the borrower expects repayment cash to arrive. The business may have regular monthly revenue, seasonal receipts or a specific contract payment. A digital intake should not treat gross monthly sales as money freely available for a new payment. It can invite the applicant to provide basic costs and existing obligations, with a clear explanation that full underwriting will occur later. For some product categories, a human reviewer may need to interpret a seasonal pattern rather than reject it from a rigid form.

The aim is not to build a longer questionnaire. It is to ask a few high-value questions in the right order. A good first screen can capture purpose, amount and deadline. Subsequent questions can adapt to the answer. If the borrower selects an asset purchase, request asset and seller details. If the answer is an operating gap, ask about receivables and repayment dates. Keep the path short enough to finish but complete enough to avoid misleading matches.

Show why a path is suggested

When an interface proposes a financing category, explain the signal behind it. “You described a vehicle purchase with a seller invoice, so equipment or vehicle financing may be worth reviewing” is more useful than “Recommended for you.” Also state what remains unknown: eligibility, price, approval, security and actual availability. The user should be able to see other paths and correct any mistaken answer.

Do not rank products by commission or a headline approval speed while presenting the result as neutral guidance. If a platform receives referral compensation, disclose it plainly. If only some lenders are included, say so. A suggested category is not an offer, and a match is not a promise that the borrower's file will pass review. The FTC's small-business financing guidance underscores the importance of accurate representations about funding and obligations.

Loanfy's existing article on designing a clear online borrower experience addresses the broader digital journey. This guide focuses on the decision before product selection: helping the borrower describe the need without pretending that a form can replace a financing conversation.

Keep the comparison honest

Borrower signalUseful next questionWhat the interface must not imply
Specific asset purchaseWhat is the quote, seller and useful life?That asset finance is automatically approved or cheapest.
Receivables arriving laterWhen are invoices due and how reliable are payments?That the expected cash is guaranteed.
Seasonal expansionWhat happens in the lowest normal month?That peak-month revenue covers a year-round obligation.
Unclear product choiceWhat problem does the money solve first?That a guessed category is a final recommendation.

A comparison should display the same dimensions across available paths: funds actually received, total cost, repayment frequency, term, collateral and guarantees, funding conditions and whether the product is available in the borrower's location. If those figures are not yet known, show “subject to offer” instead of a fabricated estimate. A product card should make uncertainty legible rather than hide it in fine print.

The Loanfy article on comparing loan offers discusses the later stage, after terms are available. The product-choice stage should prepare borrowers for that comparison by capturing clean information and explaining which documents will be requested. It should not manufacture a cost comparison before a lender has issued terms.

Design a reversible intake

Borrowers change their understanding as they answer questions. A person may begin with “working capital” and then realize the main spend is a named machine. The flow should let them move back, edit the purpose and see how the required documents change. It should save progress without treating every early selection as a formal application. Clear labels can distinguish “explore options,” “request a match,” “prequalify” and “apply,” including when credit may be checked.

Data minimization matters. Ask for sensitive details only when they are needed for the next legitimate step, explain who receives them and protect them according to the platform's actual practices. If the site cannot determine product fit, offer a human review rather than forcing an arbitrary result. An honest “we need more information” is better than a confident but unsupported match.

For teams implementing such flows, borrower portals can keep requests and documents organized while document automation can reduce repetitive follow-up. The software's job is to preserve the borrower's explanation and make the lender review clearer, not to declare a financing decision before underwriting.

Review the handoff to a human

The intake summary should give the reviewer the borrower's words, the use-of-funds schedule, deadline, asset or receivable documents, and unanswered questions. Do not reduce the whole story to a product tag and monthly revenue field. A useful reviewer can spot when an apparent equipment request includes a separate cash-flow need, or when a proposed short-term product conflicts with the life of an asset.

Tell the borrower what will happen next, who may contact them, what documents may be requested and what has not yet been decided. Then measure whether the interface actually reduces misrouted applications, repeated document requests and abandoned sessions. A single Reddit story can inspire a design hypothesis; real product decisions need testing with the platform's own users and data.

Frequently asked questions

Should a loan form require the borrower to pick a product first?

Not always. Asking about purpose, timing and repayment source can produce a better starting point, especially when the borrower is unsure of product labels.

Can a platform recommend a loan type without underwriting?

It can explain categories that may be worth discussing, with clear limits. It should not represent a category suggestion as approval, eligibility or final terms.

Is a fast application always a better borrower experience?

No. A short form that misroutes the borrower or hides costs can create more work later. Ask the minimum useful questions and explain the next step accurately.

Does the Reddit post prove how most borrowers behave?

No. It is one public account of confusion. A lending team should validate any broader product hypothesis with its own research and observed customer journey.
